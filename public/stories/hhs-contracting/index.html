Medicare’s machinery runs on contractors

HHS has obligated $170.5 billion for professional services in this dataset. Almost half went to the agency that runs Medicare and Medicaid. Here’s who does that work, and which big contracts are about to be competed again.

By Peter Hagen, Recompete Radar. Data as of Nov. 4, 2026.

The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services pays out well over a trillion dollars a year in benefits with a staff of about 6,000. Nearly everything else, from processing claims to running healthcare.gov to answering 1‑800‑MEDICARE, is done by contractors.

That shows up as a lopsided pattern. Switch between dollars and number of awards below: CMS barely registers by count, but it dwarfs every other part of HHS by money.

No single company runs Medicare

You might expect one giant integrator to dominate. Instead, CMS spreads its work across more than 900 vendors. The largest, General Dynamics IT, holds about 8% of the agency’s contract dollars.

How this was made

Award data comes from USAspending.gov, limited to professional services industry codes (IT, consulting, engineering and research), and processed through the Recompete Radar pipeline in dbt on Snowflake. Dollars are lifetime obligations per award. A contract counts as truly ending when its current end date is within three months of its latest possible end date. Descriptions of the work are inferred from industry codes and public reporting, since award descriptions aren’t yet in the pipeline.
