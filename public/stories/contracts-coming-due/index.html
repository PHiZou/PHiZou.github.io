$62.9 billion in federal work comes up for grabs in the next 12 months

At 20 civilian agencies, 11,794 professional services contracts reach the end of their final period by November 2027, with no options left to extend them. Here is when they end, who holds them, and how much of it belongs to small businesses.

By Peter Hagen · Data as of Nov. 8, 2026

Most federal service contracts run in one-year option periods. A contract can look like it is ending when the agency is really just about to renew it for another year. This story only counts contracts in their last period, where the agency has to compete the work again, extend it in some other way, or let it lapse.

That leaves a large, very uneven calendar. Use the buttons to switch between dollars and number of contracts.

Big companies hold most of the money

Matching each contract holder to its SAM.gov registration shows whether it counts as a small business in the industry the contract is classified under. Many firms are small in some industries and large in others, so that distinction matters.

Two departments dominate

Health and Human Services, which runs Medicare and Medicaid, has the most money ending. Veterans Affairs is close behind, and almost all of its dollars sit with large companies.

The biggest single contracts

The largest contract ending is the one to run the Oak Ridge Institute for Science and Education, worth $3.2 billion over its life. But the biggest cluster is at Veterans Affairs, where the multi-year effort to replace the department's electronic health record has several large contracts finishing at once.

What agencies are asking for right now

SAM.gov lists the solicitations and early notices agencies have open today. These don't map one to one onto the contracts above, since a new solicitation rarely names the contract it replaces. But they show where the next round of work is already moving.

Across every kind of purchase, nearly half of open notices at these agencies are reserved for small businesses. For professional services, it's a quarter. That matches the contracts ending: in services, most of the money goes to firms that compete without a set-aside.

How this was made

Contract data comes from USAspending.gov, limited to professional services at 20 civilian agencies (Defense is not included), and processed in a dbt pipeline on Snowflake. A contract counts as in its final period when its current end date is within three months of its latest possible end date, so no options are left. The window is Nov. 8, 2026 to Nov. 7, 2027. Dollar figures are lifetime obligations on each contract. Company size comes from the public SAM.gov entity registration file, matched on each company's unique entity ID and the contract's industry code. Open notices come from SAM.gov's daily contract opportunities file.
